Preflight history: three layouts

All three sit inside the design-A shell, under the tab bar Dashboard | Handbook | Stories | Preflight. They show /p/coins/preflight with real data: all 36 rows of coins' preflight-cost.csv (the old shape, with no project column), read by header name and listed newest first. Blank cells show "—", wall time is m:ss, tokens are in + out in compact form, and any audit tier other than sonnet (11 of the 36) carries a "check the tier" flag. Each page has working filters (mode, branch contains, where), a trend strip drawn as inline SVG with hover read-outs, and the empty state and "2 rows could not be read" note shown small at the bottom.

The trend figures tell a clear story. In the last 30 days there were 25 runs with a median of 13:46 and 3.0M tokens. In the 30 days before, there were 11 runs with a median of 24:52 and 12.8M tokens. Test counts and failures are not in the CSV yet, so no option can show them; they arrive with backlog F-5. Things to try: choose Scoped (15 runs), type gs- into the branch box, set Where to Worktrees (coins has none), and hover a chart.

Option A: dense ledger

The filters come first, then a trend strip with the three figures and the two line charts side by side, then one wide table with all twelve columns: every column in the story plus a greyed, hatched Tests · needs F-5 placeholder. The header stays in view as you scroll the table, and hovering a row marks that run on both charts.

Good: the most literal match for "a table I can go down". Every figure is visible at once and easy to compare down a column. When F-5 lands, it fills a column that is already there.

Costs: at 36 rows it is a wall of numbers, so how fast things are moving has to come from the charts. On a phone, the table scrolls sideways inside its box.

Option B: timeline

Runs are grouped by week (or by day, with a toggle) along a vertical rule, and each week's header gives its run count and medians. Each run is a card with its date, branch, mode, where and tier chips, the smaller figures in one line, and two bars for wall time and tokens. The bars share one scale across the page, and each has a tick at the median. A compact trend strip sits on top.

Good: progression reads straight down the page. Bars get shorter from August to September and nothing needs to be compared by eye. It works on a phone without any sideways scroll.

Costs: it is the tallest of the three at about 36 cards, and turns, tool calls and pack sit in small text, not in columns you can scan. This layout is least like a "table", and test data would need a new line on each card.

Option C: split view

The left panel stays in place and holds the three figures (now, before and the change), the two charts stacked, and counts by audit tier and by mode. The right side is a short table showing when, branch, mode, wall time, tokens and tier. Click a run and it opens in place, with every field, how it compares to the median ("1.5× median"), a subagent-share bar, a note on the tier, and a dashed "tests · failures — needs F-5" box. The open run stays marked on the charts.

Good: the charts stay in view while you go down the table, and the table is narrow enough to fit a phone without sideways scroll. Details are there when you want them. The tier counts (sonnet 9, blank 16, opus+sonnet 8…) show the F-3 problem at a glance.

Costs: turns, tool calls, subagent share and pack are one click away, not in a column, so you cannot scan them down the page. On a phone, the panel stacks above the table and pushes the runs down.

The same in every option

  • Filters sit in one row above everything, and the figures, charts and runs all follow them. They are Alpine only, with no server call.
  • Wall time and tokens are two charts, one series each, never one chart with two y-axes. A dashed line marks the start of the last 30 days, which the figures compare across.
  • "check the tier" uses the amber warning tone with its words, so it never relies on colour alone. The flag says check, not wrong, because of F-3. A blank tier shows "—" and gets no flag.
  • Every coins run is in the main checkout, so Where reads "main checkout" on each row, and choosing Worktrees shows "No runs match". story-board's sb-10 and sb-15 worktree rows would show their worktree name there.
