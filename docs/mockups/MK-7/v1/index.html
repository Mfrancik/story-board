Setting up a campaign: TrueView Tuesday

Campaigns replace the bare slots. A campaign has a name, a schedule (weekly Tue 19:00, start, optional end), topics from your idea menu, an image rule (photos from the project's photo library, or text only), a one-line brief for the AI, and how far ahead to prepare. Copy rules and your taste (likes and dislikes) apply to every campaign and show here read-only. All three options use the same rules and the same real data: the StackerMint idea menu, the kit's coin photos, and the calendar from MK-10 option A as of Mon 12 Oct. Each one shows the Next 3 posts preview (date, idea, photo, when the AI drafts), the low-photo warning, the ghosts on the calendar, and the First two weeks starter pack. Every page works at 375px.

Scope change, 12 Oct: content items and the post designer are deferred, so there is no Items screen and no template field-filling. The AI writes the caption and picks a photo from the library, or proposes a text-only post. A disabled “Connect a data source” box marks where coins' real collection plugs in later. For now the Marketing sub-nav is Library · Review · Calendar · Campaigns · Settings.

AList + one-page form everything on one page, preview pinned

A Campaigns page lists every campaign in one row each, with a starter-pack card underneath. Editing opens a dedicated page with five numbered sections (Name, When, Topics, Image, Voice) on the left. On the right, pinned, are the campaign in one line, the Next 3 posts and a five-week mini calendar. Every change updates all three at once: the ghosts turn purple until you save. This option is fastest for editing an existing campaign, because you see and change everything without stepping through. It follows the canonical “complex create/edit = dedicated page” pattern.

COn the calendar campaigns are cards in the calendar-A tray

This builds directly on your calendar pick (MK-10 A). The tray's “Recurring slots” becomes Campaigns: each card shows its schedule, source and on/off switch, with the starter pack below. Clicking a card, or any of its ghosts, swaps the tray for an editor with collapsible sections. The month stays in view, and the campaign's ghosts move as you change the day or time. They show purple while unsaved, then turn amber dashed. Below 1180px the editor becomes a full-height slide-over, and phones get the agenda list. This option is best for seeing the effect on your week, with no separate Campaigns page to visit.

Gaps and questions

  • Photo tags. The image rule filters the photo library by tag (toned, morgan, key date…). MK-2's photo library has no tags yet, so this needs a tags column and a way to tag on upload. Uploads made from a campaign get that campaign's tags.
  • “Used” for photos and ideas. “Not used in 90 days” counts posted, scheduled and in-Review posts. That needs a link from each post to its library photo and to the idea it came from. Should a skipped candidate free its photo again? The mockups assume yes.
  • The idea menu as data. Topics are the kit's idea menu (8 categories here; Calendar hooks left out because it needs dates). MK-3's import would load them into an ideas table per project. Ideas with placeholders like [set] or [key date] can't be filled until a data source exists. Should the AI skip them for now, or write around them?
  • Low-photo warning. It fires when fewer than 3 unused photos match, worded as you asked: “Only 2 unused photos tagged toned, so upload more or allow repeats.” With “AI decides: photo or text” it adds that the AI goes text-only until then. Is 3 the right threshold, or should it equal the prepare-ahead weeks?
  • Changing a campaign's day. Posts already scheduled or in Review keep their own day and time. Only the open dates (ghosts) move. Confirm this.
  • When the AI drafts. Dates inside the prepare-ahead window get candidates on save; later dates get them when they enter the window (“AI proposes candidates on Tue 13 Oct”). That needs a daily scheduled job, which MK-8 owns.
  • Text-only posts. They need a brand text card to render (MK-5). The designer is deferred, so the kit's existing card styles stand in for now.
  • Templates. “Allowed templates” is gone with the designer. When it returns, it fits back in as an extra section under Image without changing the layout of any option.
